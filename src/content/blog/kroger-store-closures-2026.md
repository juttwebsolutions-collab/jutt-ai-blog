# Kroger Store Closures 2026: Full List, Reasons & What Shoppers Need to Know

Kroger is closing stores across the United States as the supermarket giant continues reshaping its store network.

The closures are part of a broader effort to improve the company's financial performance and focus resources on locations and markets where Kroger sees stronger long-term potential.

For shoppers, however, a store closure is about much more than a business decision.

It can mean a longer drive for groceries, changes to pharmacy access, job uncertainty for employees and major changes for neighborhoods that have depended on the same supermarket for years.

So, **which Kroger stores are closing, why is Kroger closing them, and what should shoppers expect next?**

Here is the latest information.

## Why Is Kroger Closing Stores?

Kroger's current closure strategy is focused largely on underperforming locations.

The company previously announced a plan to close approximately **60 stores over an 18-month period** as part of a broader effort to optimize its store footprint. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

The goal is not simply to shrink Kroger.

The company is also investing in stores and markets where it expects stronger growth.

That means Kroger's strategy is better described as **repositioning its store network** rather than abandoning physical grocery stores altogether.

In fact, Kroger continues to open new stores in growing communities.

For example, the company has been expanding in North Texas, including a new large-format Marketplace location in Aubrey that opened in July 2026. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

## How Many Kroger Stores Are Closing?

Kroger's original plan called for approximately **60 store closures over 18 months**.

However, Kroger has not released a single comprehensive public list identifying every location that will close.

That makes tracking the closures more complicated.

Fast Company reported in March that its review of local reports and other available information indicated that roughly 33 stores had already closed, with additional closures confirmed during 2026. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

## Why Are Grocery Stores Closing?

Kroger's situation is part of a larger change happening across the American grocery industry.

Supermarkets face pressure from several directions.

### 1. Changing Consumer Habits

Consumers have more options than ever.

People can shop at traditional supermarkets, warehouse clubs, discount stores, online retailers and delivery services.

That makes it harder for every individual supermarket location to maintain strong sales.

### 2. Rising Operating Costs

Running a large grocery store requires significant expenses.

These can include:

- employee wages
- utilities
- rent
- insurance
- maintenance
- security
- refrigeration
- transportation
- inventory costs

A location with weak sales can become increasingly difficult to justify financially.

### 3. Stronger Competition

Kroger competes with Walmart, Costco, Aldi, Lidl, Publix, H-E-B, Target and many regional grocery chains.

In some markets, customers have multiple alternatives within a relatively short distance.

### 4. Store Location Matters

A store can perform poorly even when the overall grocery chain is doing well.

Population changes, traffic patterns, nearby competition and neighborhood demographics can all affect whether a particular location remains profitable.

That means a closure does not automatically mean every employee will permanently lose their job.

## What Happens to Customers After a Kroger Closes?

Customers typically have several options.

They can:

- shop at another nearby Kroger
- use another Kroger-owned banner
- switch to a competing supermarket
- use grocery pickup or delivery
- change where they purchase pharmacy services

The impact can be much larger in areas where the closing supermarket is one of only a few full-service grocery stores.

That is why grocery-store closures can become a community issue rather than simply a retail story.

## Could a Closed Kroger Become Another Grocery Store?

Yes.

A closed supermarket building does not necessarily remain empty forever.

A different grocery chain can eventually lease or purchase the property.

One recent example comes from Houston.

Sprouts Farmers Market is expected to take over a former Kroger location at **9325 Katy Freeway in Hedwig Village**, although the move had not been officially confirmed by Sprouts at the time of reporting.

The Kroger location had previously been shuttered as part of Kroger's broader efficiency strategy. :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}

This demonstrates how a supermarket closure can sometimes create an opportunity for another grocery company to enter the market.

## Is Kroger Going Out of Business?

**No.**

The current store closures should not be interpreted as Kroger going out of business.

Kroger remains one of the largest grocery retailers in the United States and continues to operate thousands of stores across its various brands.

The company is simultaneously closing weaker locations and investing in new stores and growing markets.

That is very different from a company-wide shutdown.
