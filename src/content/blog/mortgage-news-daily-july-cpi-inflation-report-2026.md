# Mortgage News Daily: July CPI Inflation Report and What It Means for Mortgage Rates

The latest U.S. **July CPI inflation report** is now giving mortgage and housing markets another important signal about where interest rates could go next.

The Consumer Price Index increased **0.1% in July**, matching economists' expectations after falling 0.4% in June. On a yearly basis, consumer inflation came in at **3.4%**, down from 3.5% in June. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

For mortgage borrowers, however, the most important question is not simply whether inflation went up or down.

The bigger question is:

**What does today's CPI data mean for mortgage rates and the Federal Reserve?**

## July CPI Report: The Key Numbers

The July inflation report contained several important figures.

| CPI Measure | July 2026 |
|---|---:|
| Monthly CPI | **+0.1%** |
| Annual CPI | **3.4%** |
| Monthly Core CPI | **+0.2%** |
| Annual Core CPI | **2.5%** |
| June Annual CPI | 3.5% |

The 0.1% monthly increase was exactly what economists surveyed by Reuters had expected.

Core CPI, which excludes food and energy, increased 0.2% during July and was up 2.5% from a year earlier. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

That relatively moderate core reading is particularly important for the Federal Reserve.

## Why CPI Matters for Mortgage Rates

Mortgage rates don't directly follow the CPI report.

Instead, mortgage rates are influenced by a combination of:

- Treasury yields
- Federal Reserve expectations
- Inflation expectations
- Economic growth
- Mortgage-backed securities
- Investor demand

However, CPI data can move financial markets because investors use inflation information to estimate what the Federal Reserve might do with interest rates.

A hotter-than-expected inflation report could push bond yields higher and potentially put upward pressure on mortgage rates.

A softer inflation report can have the opposite effect.

The July report was relatively mild, which helped reduce some expectations for an immediate Fed rate increase. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

## Mortgage Rates After the CPI Report

The housing market remains under pressure from elevated borrowing costs.

Recent data showed the average 30-year mortgage rate at around **6.69% in July**, while the median existing-home price reached approximately **$434,100**. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

That combination remains challenging for buyers.

Even if inflation continues to cool, mortgage rates may not fall quickly.

The bond market still needs to become more confident that inflation is moving sustainably toward the Federal Reserve's target.

## Did July CPI Make a September Fed Rate Hike Less Likely?

The July CPI report appears to have reduced some pressure for an immediate rate increase.

After the report, market expectations for a September Fed hike declined. Reuters reported that rate markets reduced the probability of a September increase to around 40%, compared with 54% a week earlier. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

However, the September decision is not guaranteed.

The Federal Reserve will receive additional economic data before its September meeting.

That means today's CPI report is important, but it is not the final word.

## Shelter Inflation Remains Important

Housing costs remain one of the most important parts of the inflation picture.

Shelter costs increased modestly in July, while rent and owners' equivalent rent increased about 0.3% from the previous month. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

This matters because housing represents a large portion of the CPI calculation.

If shelter inflation continues slowing, it could make the overall inflation picture more comfortable for policymakers.

For mortgage markets, however, the relationship is complicated.

Mortgage rates affect housing affordability, while housing costs simultaneously influence inflation measurements.

## Gasoline Prices Helped July CPI

Energy prices also played an important role in the July report.

Gasoline prices fell **2.9% during July**, providing some relief to consumers.

However, gasoline prices were still substantially higher than they were a year earlier. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

This shows why investors need to look beyond the headline CPI number.

Energy prices can move quickly and can have a significant impact on the overall inflation reading.

## What About Core Inflation?

Core CPI is particularly important because it removes food and energy prices, which can be volatile.

July core CPI increased 0.2% month over month and 2.5% year over year.

The annual core reading was down from 2.6% in June. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

That is encouraging for inflation watchers.

But 2.5% is still above the Federal Reserve's 2% inflation goal.

Therefore, the Fed still has a reason to remain cautious.

## What Mortgage Buyers Should Watch Next

The July CPI report is only one piece of the puzzle.

Several upcoming reports could influence mortgage rates and the Fed's September decision.

### August PCE Inflation Report

The Personal Consumption Expenditures price index is scheduled for August 26.

PCE is generally considered the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation measure.

Markets will therefore pay close attention to the next PCE release. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}

### August Employment Report

The August jobs report is scheduled for September 4.

The labor market has become another major consideration for policymakers.

A weaker labor market could make the Fed more hesitant to raise rates.

### August CPI Report

The next CPI report is scheduled for September 10, just days before the Federal Reserve's September 15–16 meeting. :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}

That could make the next CPI release extremely important for mortgage markets.

## What Does This Mean for Homebuyers?

For people shopping for a home right now, the latest CPI report is cautiously positive.

It does not guarantee lower mortgage rates.

But moderate inflation reduces some of the pressure that could otherwise force the Fed toward higher interest rates.

Homebuyers should therefore watch mortgage rates rather than assuming that one CPI report will immediately change borrowing costs.

If rates decline, even a relatively small reduction can make a noticeable difference in monthly payments.

## What Does This Mean for Homeowners Looking to Refinance?

Existing homeowners may also want to monitor the inflation and bond-market reaction.

A refinance generally becomes more attractive when available mortgage rates fall sufficiently below the borrower's current rate to offset closing costs.

The July CPI report could help if it contributes to lower Treasury yields and softer mortgage pricing.

But borrowers should compare actual mortgage offers rather than relying solely on headlines.

## Mortgage News Daily Outlook

The current picture is mixed but somewhat more favorable than a hotter inflation report would have been.

Inflation is still above the Fed's target, but the July CPI report showed moderation.

The annual CPI rate declined from 3.5% to 3.4%, while annual core inflation fell from 2.6% to 2.5%. :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}

At the same time, mortgage rates remain elevated and housing affordability continues to be a major challenge.

The next few economic reports will therefore be critical.

## Final Verdict

The latest **Mortgage News Daily and CPI story** is relatively straightforward:

**July inflation did not come in hot enough to increase immediate pressure on the Federal Reserve.**

Headline CPI rose just 0.1% in July, while annual inflation slowed to 3.4%.

Core CPI increased 0.2% monthly and 2.5% annually. :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}

For mortgage borrowers, that is cautiously encouraging.

However, mortgage rates are unlikely to fall simply because CPI came in as expected.

The bond market, Federal Reserve expectations, employment data and the upcoming PCE and August CPI reports will all matter.

For now, homebuyers should watch mortgage rates closely while keeping an eye on the next major inflation and employment releases.
