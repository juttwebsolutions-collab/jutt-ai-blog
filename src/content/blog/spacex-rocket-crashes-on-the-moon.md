# SpaceX Rocket Crashes on the Moon: Everything We Know So Far

A discarded SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket stage has officially crashed into the Moon, creating a rare artificial impact on the lunar surface and giving astronomers a chance to study what happens when human-made hardware collides with another world.

The object was not an active SpaceX spacecraft or a controlled lunar mission. It was the **upper stage of a Falcon 9 rocket** that had completed its original mission and remained in space after launching Firefly Aerospace's Blue Ghost lunar lander in January 2025.

After more than a year in space, the rocket stage's orbit eventually placed it on a collision course with the Moon.

The impact occurred on **August 5, 2026**, at a speed of roughly **5,400 mph**, according to reports and observations following the event.

# What Happened to the SpaceX Rocket?

The object that crashed into the Moon was a **spent Falcon 9 upper stage** left over from a 2025 SpaceX launch.

The Falcon 9 had launched Firefly Aerospace's Blue Ghost Mission 1 on January 15, 2025. The mission successfully sent the lunar lander toward the Moon, but the Falcon 9 upper stage remained in space after completing its role in the launch.

Instead of returning to Earth, the upper stage continued traveling through space.

Over time, gravitational interactions involving Earth, the Moon and the Sun changed the object's trajectory. Astronomers tracked the rocket stage and eventually determined that its path would intersect with the lunar surface.

The object was identified as **2025-010D**, a Falcon 9 upper stage associated with the Blue Ghost mission.

# When Did the SpaceX Rocket Hit the Moon?

The Falcon 9 upper stage hit the Moon on **August 5, 2026**.

Astronomers had predicted the impact for months before the event. As the date approached, researchers refined their calculations to estimate the timing and location more accurately.

The predicted impact time was approximately **06:34 UTC on August 5**.

Because the impact happened on the Moon rather than near Earth, there was no danger to people on Earth.

The event was closely monitored by astronomers, NASA and other researchers because a confirmed human-made impact on the lunar surface provides a valuable opportunity for scientific observation.

# Why Did the Falcon 9 Rocket Crash Into the Moon?

The rocket did not intentionally launch toward the Moon.

The impact happened because the spent upper stage remained in a complex orbit after completing its original mission.

Objects traveling through the Earth-Moon system can have their trajectories changed by gravity. Small changes accumulated over time can eventually produce a completely different future path.

In this case, the Falcon 9 upper stage gradually ended up on a trajectory that intersected with the Moon.

Astronomers had been tracking the object and used orbital calculations to determine that a lunar impact was likely.

This is why headlines describing a **"SpaceX rocket crashing into the Moon"** are technically accurate, but the event should not be confused with a failed SpaceX launch or an active spacecraft losing control.

# Where Did the Rocket Hit the Moon?

# What Is Lunar Ejecta?

When something crashes into the Moon at high speed, material from the lunar surface can be thrown outward.

This material is called **ejecta**.

Ejecta can include:

- Lunar dust
- Soil
- Small rocks
- Fragments of the impactor
- Material from deeper layers of the lunar surface

Because the Moon has extremely weak atmospheric conditions compared with Earth, this material can travel through ballistic trajectories before falling back to the surface.

Studying the ejecta can therefore tell scientists about the impact itself and the layers of lunar material exposed by the collision.
