# Warren Buffett: The Investor Behind Berkshire Hathaway

**Warren Buffett** is one of the most famous investors in the world and the longtime leader associated with **Berkshire Hathaway**.

For decades, Buffett became known for buying high-quality businesses, holding investments for long periods and focusing on the underlying value of a company instead of short-term market movements.

His approach helped turn Berkshire Hathaway into one of the world's most closely watched business organizations.

As of 2026, Berkshire Hathaway has entered a new leadership era. **Gregory E. Abel became Berkshire's Chief Executive Officer on January 1, 2026, while Warren Buffett remains Chairman of the Board.** :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

## Who Is Warren Buffett?

Warren Edward Buffett is an American investor and businessman best known for his leadership of Berkshire Hathaway.

Buffett developed an interest in business and investing at a very young age.

His investment philosophy was heavily influenced by Benjamin Graham, one of the pioneers of value investing.

Over time, Buffett developed his own approach, focusing on companies with strong businesses, capable management and attractive long-term economics.

Rather than constantly buying and selling stocks, he became famous for thinking like a business owner.

## Warren Buffett and Berkshire Hathaway

**Berkshire Hathaway** is the company most closely associated with Warren Buffett.

Buffett began acquiring shares of Berkshire Hathaway in the 1960s when it was originally a struggling textile business.

Instead of continuing to focus on textiles, Buffett gradually transformed Berkshire into a diversified holding company.

Today, Berkshire has interests across a wide range of businesses, including insurance, railroads, energy, manufacturing, retail and investments.

The company owns or controls major businesses while also holding significant investments in publicly traded companies.

## Warren Buffett's Investing Philosophy

Buffett's investment philosophy is often described as **value investing**.

The basic idea is to look for businesses whose underlying value is greater than what the market price suggests.

However, Buffett's approach goes beyond simply finding cheap stocks.

He has frequently emphasized the importance of:

- Strong businesses
- Durable competitive advantages
- Honest and capable management
- Predictable earnings
- Long-term growth potential
- Sensible valuations
- Financial strength

The goal is to own excellent businesses rather than simply chase stocks that might rise quickly.

## Why Warren Buffett Thinks Long Term

One of Buffett's most recognizable characteristics is his long-term approach.

Many investors focus on daily or weekly price movements.

Buffett generally focuses on what a business could look like years or decades into the future.

This philosophy allows Berkshire Hathaway to avoid making decisions based entirely on short-term market emotions.

The approach also encourages investors to think about stocks as ownership interests in real companies.

## Berkshire Hathaway's Business Model

Berkshire Hathaway is different from a traditional single-industry company.

It operates as a diversified collection of businesses.

Its operations have included major interests in areas such as:

- Insurance
- Railroads
- Utilities
- Energy
- Manufacturing
- Retail
- Consumer products
- Services
- Investments

This diversification has helped Berkshire build a business structure that is less dependent on a single industry.

## Berkshire Hathaway Insurance Business

Insurance has historically been one of the most important parts of Berkshire Hathaway.

The company's insurance operations generate premiums and provide capital that can be invested.

This model has been extremely important to Berkshire's growth.

Insurance companies collect premiums before claims are paid, creating what is commonly known as **insurance float**.

Berkshire has used its insurance operations and investment capabilities as important parts of its overall financial strategy.

## Berkshire Hathaway Investments

Berkshire Hathaway is also famous for its large portfolio of publicly traded companies.

Buffett and his investment team have historically looked for businesses with strong brands, competitive advantages and attractive long-term economics.

The portfolio has included major companies across sectors such as consumer products, financial services, technology and energy.

The exact holdings can change over time, so investors should always check Berkshire Hathaway's latest regulatory filings for current information.

## Warren Buffett's Legacy

Buffett's influence extends far beyond Berkshire Hathaway.

Millions of investors have studied his letters to shareholders, annual meeting discussions and investing philosophy.

His approach has encouraged people to focus on business fundamentals instead of constantly reacting to market noise.

One of his most important lessons is that successful investing does not necessarily require constant activity.

Patience, discipline and understanding the businesses being purchased can be more important than trying to predict every short-term market movement.

## Warren Buffett's Most Important Investing Lessons

Several principles are commonly associated with Buffett's investing approach.

### Buy Quality Businesses

A strong business can continue creating value for many years.

### Think Like an Owner

Investors should understand that buying shares means owning part of a business.

### Avoid Emotional Decisions

Short-term market declines do not automatically mean that a good business has become a bad business.

### Be Patient

Long-term compounding can be more powerful than frequent trading.

### Understand What You Buy

Investors should understand a company's business model, risks and financial position before investing.
