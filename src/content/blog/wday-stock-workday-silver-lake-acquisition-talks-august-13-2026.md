# WDAY Stock Surges as Silver Lake Reportedly Eyes Workday

**Workday stock (NASDAQ: WDAY)** became one of the biggest stories in the software sector on Thursday, August 13, 2026, after reports emerged that private-equity firm **Silver Lake is in discussions to acquire Workday**.

The report immediately sent Workday shares sharply higher.

The potential transaction would be one of the biggest software buyouts in recent history if it eventually reaches an agreement. Workday's market value was reported at roughly **$43 billion** when the acquisition discussions emerged. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

However, investors need to understand one important point:

**There is no confirmed acquisition agreement yet.**

The reported discussions are ongoing, and there is no guarantee that Silver Lake and Workday will ultimately reach a deal. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

## Why Is WDAY Stock Up Today?

The immediate catalyst is the report that Silver Lake is discussing a possible acquisition of Workday.

MarketWatch reported that Workday shares jumped **17.6% on August 13**, making the stock the strongest-performing S&P 500 component during the session. It was also Workday's biggest one-day gain in more than a decade. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

The move shows how seriously investors are taking the possibility of a take-private transaction.

When a company becomes a potential acquisition target, investors often begin pricing in the possibility that a buyer could pay a premium over the current market price.

That is exactly what appears to be happening with WDAY.

## What Is Silver Lake Looking to Buy?

Workday is a major enterprise software company focused on human resources, finance and business management.

Its platform is used by thousands of organizations around the world to manage employees, payroll, financial processes and other important business operations.

The company has also been aggressively expanding its artificial intelligence capabilities.

That makes Workday particularly interesting to private-equity investors looking for established software companies with recurring subscription revenue and significant enterprise customer relationships.

## Silver Lake and Workday Have an Existing Connection

One reason the potential transaction has attracted so much attention is the relationship between Silver Lake and Workday leadership.

Workday co-founder **Aneel Bhusri** returned as chief executive earlier this year.

Silver Lake's co-CEO Egon Durban has also had a long-standing relationship with Bhusri, according to reports and market commentary surrounding the potential transaction. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

That relationship does not mean a deal is guaranteed.

But it could make discussions easier because the parties are already familiar with each other.

## Workday's Business Is Still Growing

The Silver Lake story is only part of the WDAY investment picture.

Workday's underlying business continues to generate significant revenue and cash flow.

In its fiscal 2027 first-quarter results, Workday reported:

- Total revenue of **$2.542 billion**
- Revenue growth of **13.5% year over year**
- Subscription revenue of **$2.354 billion**
- Subscription revenue growth of **14.3%**
- Free cash flow of **$616 million**
- 12-month subscription revenue backlog of **$8.806 billion**

Workday also reiterated fiscal 2027 subscription revenue guidance of approximately **$9.925 billion to $9.950 billion**. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

Those numbers are important because they show that the company was already operating as a substantial recurring-revenue software business before the Silver Lake reports appeared.

## Workday Is Betting Heavily on AI

Workday is also trying to position itself for the next stage of enterprise AI adoption.

The company says thousands of customers are already using its organically developed AI agents.

Workday reported that more than **4,000 customers** were using at least one of its agents as of its fiscal 2027 first-quarter results.

It also said its Recruiting Agent supported **14 million hiring processes** during the quarter, representing a 44% year-over-year increase. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

That AI strategy could be one of the reasons Workday remains strategically valuable.

Instead of simply being an established HR software company, Workday is attempting to become an AI platform embedded directly inside enterprise workflows.

## What Would a Silver Lake Deal Mean for WDAY Investors?

If Silver Lake eventually reaches an agreement with Workday, existing shareholders could potentially receive a premium to the stock's unaffected price.

That is one reason WDAY shares jumped so sharply after the report.

However, investors should not automatically assume that today's price represents the final acquisition value.

Several things still have to happen before shareholders can treat an acquisition as real.

The companies would need to negotiate definitive terms, conduct the required due diligence, obtain necessary approvals and complete the transaction.

Until an official agreement is announced, the acquisition remains speculation.

## What Happens If the Deal Falls Apart?

This is probably the biggest risk for traders buying WDAY after the huge rally.

If investors purchase shares expecting a takeover and Silver Lake ultimately walks away, the stock could give back some or potentially much of the takeover-related gain.

That is known as **deal-risk**.

In other words, WDAY is no longer trading purely on Workday's underlying earnings and growth expectations.

The market is also reacting to the possibility of a buyout.

If that possibility disappears, the valuation could change quickly.

## Workday's AI Business Could Still Matter Without a Deal

Even if Silver Lake does not acquire Workday, the company's AI strategy remains important.

Workday reported that it delivered **1.7 billion AI actions** across its platform during fiscal 2026.

The company also launched Sana, expanded its agent ecosystem and acquired technology designed to improve AI-agent integrations. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

That means there are actually two separate stories surrounding WDAY:

**Takeover story:** Silver Lake may acquire Workday.

**Business story:** Workday is attempting to use AI to accelerate its enterprise software business.

Investors should keep those two stories separate.

## Why Private Equity Could Be Interested in Workday

Workday has several characteristics that can be attractive to private-equity investors.

First, the company generates recurring subscription revenue.

Second, its customers are large enterprises that often have significant switching costs.

Third, Workday has strong cash generation.

Fourth, the company has an established position in HR and finance software.

Finally, its AI strategy could create additional opportunities for long-term growth.

A private-equity owner could potentially take Workday private and focus on longer-term operational changes without the same short-term pressure associated with public markets.

## WDAY Stock Outlook

The near-term outlook for WDAY stock is now dominated by the Silver Lake story.

Investors will be watching for any official announcement from Workday or Silver Lake.

The most important developments would include:

- Confirmation or denial of acquisition discussions
- Any reported offer price
- Whether a premium is attached to the current share price
- Financing details
- Workday's response
- Potential regulatory issues
- Whether negotiations progress toward a definitive agreement

Until those details become available, investors should treat the acquisition report as a potential catalyst rather than a completed transaction.

## Bull Case for WDAY

The bullish case is straightforward.

If Silver Lake eventually makes a formal offer at a significant premium, shareholders could benefit from a higher acquisition price.

At the same time, Workday's underlying business continues to grow.

Its subscription backlog remains large, free cash flow is strong and management is investing heavily in AI. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}

That combination gives investors two potential sources of upside:

**a possible takeover premium and continued business growth.**

## Bear Case for WDAY

The biggest risk is that no acquisition actually happens.

The reported talks are not a completed transaction.

If negotiations collapse, investors could quickly refocus on Workday's standalone valuation.

The company also faces the broader challenge affecting much of the software industry: investors are questioning how AI will change traditional SaaS businesses.

Workday must prove that AI will strengthen its platform rather than reduce the value of traditional enterprise software.

## What Should Investors Watch Next?

The next major catalyst is any official confirmation regarding Silver Lake.

Until then, WDAY could remain highly volatile.

Traders may focus heavily on headlines, while long-term investors may continue evaluating Workday's revenue growth, subscription backlog, margins, free cash flow and AI adoption.

The distinction is important.

A short-term trader may be focused almost entirely on the acquisition premium.

A long-term investor may care more about whether Workday can build a durable AI-powered enterprise software platform.

## Final Verdict on WDAY Stock

**WDAY stock has suddenly become one of the most important software stocks to watch after reports that Silver Lake is in talks to acquire Workday.**

The reported deal could potentially rank among the largest software buyouts in history, given Workday's roughly $43 billion market value at the time of the report. :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}

But investors should remain careful.

**Silver Lake has not completed a deal with Workday, and the discussions could still end without an agreement.**

At the same time, Workday's underlying business remains significant, with billions in subscription revenue, a large backlog, strong cash generation and an aggressive AI strategy. :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}

For now, the key question is not simply **"Will WDAY go higher?"**

The bigger question is:

**Will Silver Lake turn today's acquisition speculation into a formal offer?**

That answer could determine the next major move in Workday stock.
